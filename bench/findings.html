• The CPU is the fastest single device at every size. It reaches 1.3–1.7 GB/s while the source fits in the file cache, and about 0.8–0.9 GB/s from 30 GiB up (3 × 10 GiB, 4 × 10 GiB, all sets, the 100 GiB mix), where the one drive limits reading and writing.
  • The GPU is slow on small jobs. OpenCL start-up takes about 0.25 s and a 10 MiB file has too few chunks to fill the GPU. From 1 GiB up it holds 430–480 MB/s at 2–32 KiB chunks, the same on 1 GiB as on 100 GiB.
  • ALL only pays off on big jobs. It matches or slightly beats the CPU from 6 GiB up and is slower below that. On the 100 GiB mix it is often slower than the CPU alone, because reading 100 GiB from the one drive is the limit.
  • Small chunks are fast but save less. Each chunk starts with an empty history: on all sets together 1 KiB saves 8.86%, 2 KiB 9.46%, 8 KiB 10.15% and 32 KiB 10.59%.
  • Chunks over 64 KiB save nothing more. DEFLATE can only look back 32 KiB, so 128 KiB to 1 MiB give the same size as 64 KiB (10.63–10.67% on all sets and on the 100 GiB mix). The GPU gets slower as chunks get longer and fewer: about 475 MB/s at 8–32 KiB, 390 at 128 KiB and 335 at 256 KiB to 1 MiB. 4–16 KiB is the best balance.
  • Bigger sources do not change the speed per byte. 3 × 10 GiB, 4 × 10 GiB, all sets (43 GiB) and the 100 GiB mix all run at about the same MB/s on each device: above about 16 GiB the drive sets the limit, not the amount of data or the number of files (1,591 in the 100 GiB mix).
  • .ocpk with DEFLATE works on the GPU and writes the same archive on GPU, ALL and CPU. On all sets together it saves 18.09%, against 10.65% for ZIP and 20.00% for .ocpk XPRESS. The GPU runs it at about 450–475 MB/s, CPU and ALL at about 750–900 MB/s. The block size barely matters: 1 MiB to 64 MiB saves 18.09–18.14%.
  • .ocpk XPRESS gets worse with big blocks. On all sets together it saves 20.0% at 1 MiB, 19.6% at 8 MiB, 17.6% at 32 MiB and 15.8% at 64 MiB, because its quick sampling judges a whole mixed block at once and stores more of it raw. 1–4 MiB is best.
  • The 100 GiB mix repeats data. The Object folder holds only 48.7 GiB, so most data appears twice in this set. ZIP is not affected (10.6% saved at 32 KiB), but .ocpk stores the repeats once and saves 56–58%. With half the data stored once, .ocpk DEFLATE on the GPU reaches 590–655 MB/s there.
  • Options added later ran once, so a few single runs are slow outliers, for example ZIP CPU 256 KiB on the 100 GiB mix (188 s against about 130 s for its neighbours) and .ocpk CPU 32 MiB on all sets (96 s against about 62 s). Sizes are exact; read single-run speeds with that in mind.
