• In progress: the cells that say “not run” on 4 × 10 GiB and the 100 GiB mix are being filled, one run at a time, finishing around 22:15.
  • The 100 GiB mix (1,591 files from 1 MiB to 10 GiB) runs at the same speed per byte as the 30–43 GiB sources: CPU about 900 MB/s, GPU about 480 MB/s, ALL slower than CPU because reading 100 GiB from the one drive is the limit. The Object folder holds only 48.7 GiB, so most data appears twice in this set: ZIP is not affected (10.6% saved), but .ocpk stores the repeats once and saves 57%.
  • The CPU is the fastest single device at every size. It reaches 1.3–1.7 GB/s while the source fits in the file cache, and about 0.8–1.0 GB/s on 30–43 GiB, where the one drive limits reading and writing.
  • The GPU is slow on small jobs. OpenCL start-up takes about 0.25 s and a 10 MiB file has too few chunks to fill the GPU. From 1 GiB up it holds 430–475 MB/s at 1–32 KiB chunks.
  • ALL only pays off on big jobs. It matches or slightly beats the CPU from 6 GiB up and is slower below that.
  • Small chunks are fast but save less. Each chunk starts with an empty history: on all sets together 1 KiB saves 8.86%, 2 KiB 9.46% and 32 KiB 10.59%.
  • Chunks over 64 KiB save nothing more. DEFLATE can only look back 32 KiB, so 128 KiB to 1 MiB give the same size as 64 KiB (10.63–10.65% on all sets together). The GPU gets slower as chunks get longer and fewer: 472 MB/s at 32 KiB, 393 at 128 KiB, 333 at 512 KiB and 1 MiB. 4–16 KiB is the best balance.
  • 4 × 10 GiB behaves like 3 × 10 GiB: the speed per byte is the same (CPU about 820 MB/s, GPU about 460 MB/s), so the drive, not the amount of data, sets the limit above about 16 GiB.
  • .ocpk with DEFLATE works on the GPU and writes the same archive on GPU, ALL and CPU. On all sets together it saves 18.09%, against 10.65% for ZIP and 20.00% for .ocpk XPRESS. The GPU runs it at about 450–475 MB/s, CPU and ALL at about 750–900 MB/s. The block size barely matters: 2 MiB to 64 MiB saves 18.09–18.14%.
  • .ocpk XPRESS gets worse with big blocks. On all sets together it saves 20.0% at 1 MiB, 19.6% at 8 MiB, 17.6% at 32 MiB and 15.8% at 64 MiB, because its quick sampling judges a whole mixed block at once and stores more of it raw. 1–4 MiB is best.
  • Options added later ran once. A few single runs are slow outliers (for example .ocpk CPU 32 MiB on all sets: 96 s against about 62 s for its neighbours), so read their speed with that in mind. Sizes are exact.
